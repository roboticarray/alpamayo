## What it is

Motus is a ~8 B-parameter **unified latent-action world model** from Tsinghua's ML group: a Mixture-of-Transformers that runs three experts over a shared token stream — a video generation expert (Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, ~5.0 B), an understanding expert (~253 M) fed by a frozen Qwen3-VL-2B, and an action expert (~641 M) — with a UniDiffuser-style scheduler that lets you flip which modalities are noised and which are clean. That single mechanism is what lets one checkpoint serve as a world model, a VLA policy, an inverse-dynamics model, a plain video generator, or a joint video-action predictor. It both **consumes and produces** actions: `deploy_policy.py` takes three camera views plus a 14-D joint state and returns an action chunk of `num_video_frames * video_action_freq_ratio` joint targets alongside the predicted future frames. The headline result is closed-loop and in a physics simulator: **87.02% average success on RoboTwin 2.0** over 50 multi-task-trained tasks, reported as +15 points over X-VLA and +45 over π0.5. The distinguishing trick is optical-flow-derived **latent actions**, which lets stage-2 pretraining absorb action-free video (egocentric human footage, synthetic data) as if it were action-labelled — a six-layer "data pyramid" across three training stages. Datasets supported out of the box: RoboTwin 2.0, AC-One, Aloha-Agilex-2, LeRobot and MultiLeRobot.

## Security findings

- `models/motus.py:703` and `models/motus.py:751` — `torch.load(path, map_location='cpu')` with **no `weights_only`**, targeting `mp_rank_00_model_states.pt`, the 16 GB DeepSpeed pickle that is the *only* published checkpoint format. Upstream offers no safetensors alternative for any of the three checkpoints. Under the documented `torch==2.7.1` the default is `weights_only=True`, so this is not exploitable as installed — but the code does not say so, DeepSpeed checkpoints carry non-tensor metadata that can force the flag off, and the protection vanishes if anyone pins an older torch. Treat the 16 GB download as untrusted content.
- `utils/checkpointer.py:120` — same pattern for training checkpoints, plus it then loads `state_dict['optimizer']`, which is the part most likely to require `weights_only=False`.
- `models/motus.py:519,522` and `inference/real_world/Motus/inference_example.py:51,232` — `trust_remote_code=True` on `AutoProcessor`/`AutoConfig`/`Qwen3VLForConditionalGeneration.from_pretrained`. Qwen3-VL is natively supported in transformers 5.x, so the flag is unnecessary; leaving it on means any future push to the Qwen HF repo can execute arbitrary code in our training jobs. Remove it.
- `requirements.txt` — 24 of 26 entries are `>=` lower bounds with no upper pin, against `transformers==5.0.0rc0`, a **release candidate**. `diffusers>=0.25.0`, `accelerate>=0.25.0`, `deepspeed>=0.18.3` floating against an RC of transformers is a build that will break without warning and cannot be reproduced later. There is no lockfile. Note the torch pin lives only in the README install instructions (`torch==2.7.1 ... cu128`), not in any requirements file — so a naive `pip install -r requirements.txt` gets whatever torch pip picks, which also silently changes the `weights_only` default.
- Good: `utils/common.py:68-78` does it right — prefers safetensors via `safe_open` and falls back to `torch.load(path, weights_only=True)`. The correct pattern exists in the repo; the checkpoint loaders just do not use it.
- No `pickle.load`, no `eval(`/`exec(`, no `os.system`, no `subprocess`, no curl-pipe-sh, no hardcoded tokens or credentials anywhere in the tree.
- Two complete vendored copies of Wan 2.2 and Qwen2.5-VL under `bak/` and `inference/*/Motus/bak/`, not pinned to upstream commits. Upstream security fixes will never reach them, and they are not obviously used at inference time (the live path imports `wan.modules.model` — check which copy is on `PYTHONPATH` before trusting either).
- `wandb` is the default `report_to` in every config. Training phones home unless you set `report_to: "none"` or `WANDB_MODE=offline`.

## Physics and dynamics

- **Action space (in and out)**: `action_dim: 14`, `state_dim: 14` — bimanual **joint positions** (qpos), 7 per arm including the gripper, for the Aloha/AC-One class of platform. `DATA_FORMAT.md` confirms the on-disk representation is `qpos/*.pt` tensors. Normalisation statistics live in `data/utils/stat.json` and `inference/robotwin/Motus/utils/stat.json`. Unlike Ctrl-World (Cartesian EE) and OpenDWM (ego pose), this is a **joint-space** model, which is the representation Isaac Lab's `JointPositionActionCfg` speaks natively — no adapter needed.
- **Latent actions**: the stage-2 contribution. Optical flow between consecutive frames is quantised into a pixel-level "delta action" (`configs/latent_action.yaml`, `data/latent_action/`, `data/utils/calc_latent_action_quantiles.py`), letting the action expert train on video with no action labels. Quantile and min/max statistics for the latent space are checked into `data/utils/latent_action_q01_q99.json` and friends. This is the mechanism that would let us pretrain on unlabelled fleet video.
- **Chunking and rates**: `action_chunk_size = num_video_frames * video_action_freq_ratio` (`models/motus.py:86`). RoboTwin: 8 video frames x ratio 2 = **16 actions**; LeRobot config: 8 x 6 = **48 actions**. `global_downsample_rate: 3` on RoboTwin takes 30 Hz source data down to 10 Hz of video with actions at 20 Hz; the LeRobot config uses rate 1 with ratio 6. The video and action streams are explicitly decoupled in frequency, which is the right design and better than any other repo here.
- **Resolution / horizon**: 384x320 per sample, **three camera views concatenated into one image** (head + left wrist + right wrist) rather than modelled as separate streams — `data/utils/multi_camera_concat.py`, and `INFERENCE.md` warns in bold that inputs must be pre-concatenated. Eight predicted video frames is a short horizon: under a second of imagination per call. `num_inference_timesteps: 10` for the flow-matching sampler.
- **Evaluation**: closed-loop success rate in RoboTwin 2.0, a physics simulator, on 50 tasks trained jointly from 50 clean + 500 randomised episodes each — 87.02% average. `inference/robotwin/Motus/auto_eval.sh` and `eval.sh` run the sweep, `per_eval_logs.sh` collates it, `tasks_all.txt` lists the tasks. This is the strongest physics evidence in the batch: not FID, not a correlation study, but success rates inside a simulator that resolves contact.
- **Known failure modes**: none documented. The absence is itself a finding — `README.md`'s troubleshooting section covers only OOM and checkpoint/config mismatch. Structurally: an 8-frame horizon means long-horizon imagination requires autoregression that nothing in the repo evaluates; three-view *concatenation* means the model has no explicit cross-view geometry, so it cannot be relied on for 3D consistency; and the whole system is trained on bimanual tabletop data.

## Motion-control assessment

Motus is the repo in this batch whose architecture we would most want to own, and the licence means we actually can. A permissively-licensed 8 B model that takes three camera views plus joint state and emits both a joint-position action chunk and the predicted future is, structurally, exactly the "world-action model" slot next to Alpamayo — and unlike Genie-Envisioner (CC BY-NC-SA) or Ctrl-World (Stability revenue cap), nothing stops us from fine-tuning it, deploying it, or building a product on it.

The Isaac Lab path is the shortest of any repo reviewed, because RoboTwin 2.0 *is* a physics simulator and the adapter already exists. `inference/robotwin/Motus/deploy_policy.py` defines a clean contract: `update_obs(observation)` consuming `observation['observation'][{head,left,right}_camera]['rgb']` plus `observation['joint_action']['vector']`, then `get_action(instruction)` returning a list of arrays. Re-pointing that at an Isaac Lab `ManagerBasedRLEnv` is a mechanical substitution — grab three `TiledCamera` sensors, concatenate them with `data/utils/multi_camera_concat.py`, read `env.scene["robot"].data.joint_pos` for the 14-D state, and feed the returned chunk into a `JointPositionActionCfg` term. Because the action space is already joint position, there is no Cartesian/joint adapter to learn, which is the single biggest practical advantage over Ctrl-World. The `stat.json` normalisation would need regenerating for our robot with `data/utils/calc_stat.py`.

Two things make this more than a policy port. First, the **latent action** mechanism is directly applicable to our data problem: it turns action-free video into pretraining signal, which is what we would want for absorbing fleet footage or Unreal renders that have no proprioception. Second, the UniDiffuser-style mode switching means the same checkpoint gives us an inverse-dynamics model — feed two observations, get the action between them — which is a genuinely useful tool for labelling demonstration video and for sim-to-real action retargeting, and no other repo here offers it.

What is missing. The embodiment assumption is hard: 14-D bimanual joint positions and a three-view concatenated image. For AV work there is nothing — no ego pose, no map, no multi-camera geometry, no driving data — so this does not touch Alpamayo or AlpaSim directly. The 8-frame video horizon is short enough that "world model" is doing some work in the name; it is closer to a short-horizon dynamics head on a VLA than a long-rollout simulator, and the repo evaluates no long-horizon rollout. There is no pixel-space evaluation at all (no FID/FVD/PSNR), so the quality of the generated future is unquantified. And the engineering has the shape of a fast paper release: no tests, no CI, three copies of the model, RC dependency pins, author cluster paths in every config.

## Extensibility

- **New embodiment**: copy one of `configs/{robotwin,ac_one,aloha_agilex_2,lerobot}.yaml` and set `common.action_dim`, `common.state_dim`, `common.num_video_frames`, `common.video_action_freq_ratio`, `common.global_downsample_rate`, plus `dataset.type`. Regenerate normalisation with `data/utils/calc_stat.py`. This is a genuine config-level change, not a fork.
- **New dataset**: `data/` has one subpackage per format (`robotwin2`, `ac_one`, `aloha_agilex_2`, `lerobot`, `latent_action`) selected by `dataset.type` in YAML, with `data/dataset.py` as the dispatcher. LeRobot and MultiLeRobot arrived as post-release community-facing additions, which is evidence the seam works. A RoboTwin raw-data converter ships at `data/robotwin2/robotwin_data_convert/`.
- **New expert / modality**: the MoT design (`models/{motus,action_expert,und_expert,wan_model}.py`) routes a shared token stream through per-expert transformer blocks; adding a fourth expert (depth, tactile, LiDAR) means a new module plus wiring in `models/motus.py`, and the UniDiffuser scheduler already handles which streams are noised. This is the most architecturally forward-looking extension point in the batch.
- **Action expert sizing**: `hidden_size` and `ffn_dim_multiplier` are config fields (`hidden_size` must be a multiple of 128 for `head_dim=128`), so the action head can be scaled without touching the backbone.
- **Coupling hotspots**: every config hardcodes `/share/home/bhz/pretrained_models/...` and `/share/dataset/preprocess/robotwin2`. `models/` is duplicated verbatim into `inference/robotwin/Motus/models/` and `inference/real_world/Motus/models/` (identical MD5s), so every model change must be made three times or the inference paths silently diverge from training. Three-view concatenation is a preprocessing convention, not a model input — get the view order wrong and it fails silently with degraded actions.

## Hardware and cost

The only repo here with a published VRAM table (`README.md` "Requirements"):

| Mode | VRAM | Recommended GPU |
|---|---|---|
| Inference, pre-encoded T5 | > 24 GB | RTX 5090 |
| Inference, T5 at runtime | ~ 41 GB | A100 40/80 GB, H100, B200 |
| Training | > 80 GB | A100 80 GB, H100, B200 |

Pre-encoding the instruction with `inference/real_world/Motus/encode_t5_instruction.py` is what buys the 24 GB path — the UMT5 text encoder is the difference, and RoboTwin datasets ship pre-encoded `umt5_wan/*.pt` embeddings for exactly this reason. Training uses DeepSpeed ZeRO (stage 1 and 2 configs at `configs/zero1.json`, `configs/zero2.json`), `batch_size: 8`, bf16 with AMP, `grad_clip_norm: 0.5`, up to 1 M steps. **No wall-clock or control-frequency number is published anywhere** — with 10 flow-matching steps over an 8 B MoT it should be far faster than a 50-step diffusion model, but nobody measured it, and for a policy that must close a control loop that is the number that matters. Measure it before committing.

## Verdict and next step

**ADOPT, scoped.** Motus is the only model in this batch that is Apache-2.0 from code through weights through both backbones, the only one that is simultaneously a world model, a policy and an inverse-dynamics model, the only one whose action space (14-D joint position) plugs into Isaac Lab without an adapter, and the only one whose headline number is a closed-loop success rate inside a physics simulator. The engineering is rough — no tests, no CI, three copies of the model tree, `>=` pins against a `transformers` release candidate, author cluster paths everywhere, and a 16 GB pickle as the sole checkpoint format — but all of that is fixable in a fork, whereas a non-commercial licence is not. The scope: adopt it as the reference architecture and permissive starting checkpoint for a roboticarray world-action model, not as a drop-in product component. Concrete next step, about three weeks: (1) run `inference/real_world/Motus/inference_example.py` on the 24 GB pre-encoded-T5 path to validate the stack and, crucially, **measure action-chunk latency**, which upstream never reports; (2) port `deploy_policy.py` onto an Isaac Lab `ManagerBasedRLEnv` with a bimanual asset and reproduce a handful of RoboTwin-equivalent tasks in Isaac, which tells us how much of the 87% survives a change of simulator; (3) run the latent-action pipeline (`configs/latent_action.yaml`, `data/utils/calc_latent_action_quantiles.py`) over a few hours of our own action-free video to see whether optical-flow latent actions are a viable way to pretrain on unlabelled footage — that mechanism, not the RoboTwin checkpoint, is the part with the largest option value for us. On day one of the fork: delete the two duplicate model trees, drop `trust_remote_code=True`, pin every dependency, and convert the checkpoint to safetensors locally so nothing in CI ever unpickles a 16 GB download.
