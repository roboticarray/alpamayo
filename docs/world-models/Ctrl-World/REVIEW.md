## What it is

Ctrl-World (ICLR 2026, Stanford + Tsinghua) is an action-conditioned video world model for robot manipulation whose entire reason for existing is **policy-in-the-loop evaluation in imagination**: you drop a real VLA policy (they use openpi's π0.5-DROID) into a loop with the world model and let the two drive each other for 20 seconds with no robot and no simulator. The backbone is Stable Video Diffusion with the UNet rewired for action and frame-level pose conditioning (`models/ctrl_world.py:111-141`); actions are injected through a small MLP `Action_encoder2` (`models/ctrl_world.py:72-107`) that maps a 7-D Cartesian end-effector pose per frame into the 1024-d cross-attention stream, optionally summed with a CLIP text embedding of the instruction. It jointly models the **three DROID camera views** by stacking their latents (`dataset/dataset_droid_exp33.py:176-186`), at 320x192 per view, 5 Hz, with 6 history frames and 5 predicted future frames (1 second) per interaction step. The model **consumes** actions and does not produce them — the policy is external. Four rollout modes ship and work on the 82 MB DROID subset included in the repo: replay recorded actions, keyboard teleop (`l/r/f/b/u/d/o/c`), interactive π0.5 rollout, and the paper's full 20-episode-per-task evaluation sweep. A 1.5 MB `models/action_adapter/model2_15_9.pth` is committed to convert π0.5's joint-velocity output into the Cartesian pose the world model expects.

## Security findings

- `scripts/rollout_interact_pi.py:66`, `rollout_interact_pi_eval.py:66`, `rollout_replay_traj.py:64`, `rollout_key_board.py:64`, `scripts/train_wm.py:42,148` — `torch.load(path)` with **no `weights_only` argument**, applied to the 9.3 GB `checkpoint-10000.pt` downloaded from Hugging Face. The pinned `torch==2.7.1` defaults `weights_only=True`, so this is not exploitable as pinned — but the safety is accidental, and anyone who relaxes the pin below 2.6 silently gets arbitrary code execution from a downloaded file. There is no safetensors option: upstream publishes only the `.pt`.
- `models/action_adapter/model2_15_9.pth` — a 1.5 MB **pickled torch checkpoint committed into the git repository**, loaded at `scripts/rollout_interact_pi.py:79`. Committed binaries are the classic supply-chain vector; `train2.py` sits next to it, so regenerate it locally rather than loading the shipped one.
- `dataset/dataset_droid_exp33.py:76` — `torch.load(file)` on `.pt` latent video files. These are produced locally by `dataset_example/extract_latent.py`, so it is fine as long as you never accept someone else's pre-extracted latent cache.
- `requirements.txt` — only 3 of 13 entries are pinned (`diffusers==0.34.0`, `transformers==4.48.1`, `torch==2.7.1`). `mediapy`, `wandb`, `swanlab`, `accelerate`, `tqdm`, `numpy`, `decord`, `einops`, `scipy`, `pandas` all float, and the file ends without a trailing newline. No lockfile, no hashes. `accelerate` floating against a pinned `torch` is a known breakage source.
- `wandb` and `swanlab` (a Chinese analytics service) are both imported for training logging. Training will attempt to phone home unless `WANDB_MODE=offline` is set, as the README's own smoke-test command does.
- `config.py:11-14,136-220` — twelve hardcoded `/cephfs/...` cluster paths as dataclass defaults. Not a vulnerability, but every one of them is a silent misconfiguration waiting to happen; running a script without overriding them fails late, after model load.
- **Clean**: no `pickle.load`, no `eval(`/`exec(`, no `os.system`, no `subprocess`, no `trust_remote_code`, no curl-pipe-sh, no API keys or tokens anywhere in the tree.

## Physics and dynamics

- **Action space (input only)**: 7-D — Cartesian end-effector pose (xyz + orientation) concatenated with a gripper scalar, min-max normalised to [-1, 1] against per-dataset 1st/99th percentiles from `dataset_meta_info/droid/stat.json` (`dataset/dataset_droid_exp33.py:189-194`, `normalize_bound` in each rollout script). The model does **not** output actions. Note the conditioning vector covers *both* history and future: `action_cond.shape == (num_history + num_frames, action_dim)` = (11, 7), so the model sees where the arm has been and where it is being told to go.
- **Policy adapter**: π0.5-DROID emits joint velocities, Ctrl-World wants Cartesian pose, so a learned 3-layer `Dynamics(action_dim=7, action_num=15, hidden_size=512)` bridges them (`scripts/rollout_interact_pi.py:75-79`, trained by `models/action_adapter/train2.py`). Anyone plugging in a different policy needs their own adapter — this is the integration seam.
- **Horizon / resolution / fps**: 320x192 per view, three views jointly. `down_sample=3` takes DROID's 15 Hz to **5 Hz**. Each interaction step conditions on 6 history frames (`history_idx = [0,0,-12,-9,-6,-3]`, i.e. a ~2.4 s receding window) and generates 5 future frames = 1 second. `interact_num = 12` chains twelve of these autoregressively for a 12 s rollout; the VLAW follow-up demonstrates 20 s. `num_inference_steps = 50`, `guidance_scale = 1.0` (CFG effectively off at inference despite 5% action dropout during training).
- **Evaluation**: the repo ships the harness (`scripts/rollout_interact_pi_eval.py`, 20 episodes per task from `dataset_example/droid_new_setup_full`) but **no numbers** — those live in the paper. The claimed contribution is that success rates measured in imagination correlate with real-world success rates for instruction following. There is no FID/FVD, no trajectory error, no collision or contact metric in the code.
- **Known failure modes**: stated plainly by the authors in the README — "can be imprecise in modeling physical interactions", and the VLAW section admits the pretrained model "may not [be] accurate enough in contact-rich or deformable object tasks", which is why post-training exists. Per-task hacks betray the same weakness: `gripper_max_dict` clamps the gripper differently for each of eight task types and `z_min_dict` floors the end-effector height, i.e. the model needs the action distribution fenced in to stay coherent (`config.py:69-71`).

## Motion-control assessment

The valuable artifact here is not the model, it is the **loop**. `scripts/rollout_interact_pi.py:319-393` is a compact, readable template for: sample an initial multi-view observation → run a real VLA to get an action chunk → adapt the action representation → condition the world model → decode the next second of video → feed it back as the policy's next observation → repeat. That is precisely the evaluation architecture roboticarray would want as a *cheap pre-screen in front of Isaac Lab*: thousands of imagined rollouts to rank policy checkpoints, with only the survivors paying for a physics-accurate Isaac run. The `Dynamics` action adapter is the generalisable idea — a learned bridge between whatever representation a policy emits (joint velocity, torque) and whatever the world model was conditioned on (Cartesian pose).

For direct Isaac Lab integration the path is the reverse of Genie-Envisioner's: Ctrl-World does not run *inside* Isaac, it *replaces* it. You would post-train the checkpoint on rendered Isaac data for your own embodiment (the VLAW pipeline in section 3 of the README is exactly this: extract latents, build meta info, fine-tune) and then use the result as a fast surrogate. Concretely: render Isaac episodes to three fixed camera views at 320x192, export end-effector pose + gripper per frame, run `dataset_example/extract_latent.py`, `dataset_meta_info/create_meta_info.py`, then `scripts/train_wm.py`. Nothing in that path is DROID-specific except the dataset class, which is ~210 lines.

What is missing for us. It is manipulation-only and single-embodiment — there is nothing here for driving or AlpaSim, and the 7-D Cartesian action space does not express a vehicle trajectory or a legged gait. At 5 Hz and ~10 s of compute per 1 s of imagined video on an A100 (~5 s on H100, per the README), a 20 s rollout costs 100-200 GPU-seconds, so "thousands of rollouts" means a real GPU budget, though still far cheaper than hardware. The per-task gripper and height clamps are a warning that the model's competence envelope is narrow. And because it never emits actions, it contributes nothing to planning or control directly.

## Extensibility

- **New dataset**: copy `dataset/dataset_droid_exp33.py` (211 lines). It expects the pre-encoded latent layout produced by `dataset_example/extract_latent.py` (per-camera latent video files plus an `annotation` JSON with `states` and `action` arrays) and a `stat.json` of 1st/99th percentiles from `dataset_meta_info/create_meta_info.py`. There is no dataset registry — `dataset_names` is a string matched against directory names, and the class is imported directly.
- **New action space**: `action_dim` is a config field, but 7 is baked into the π0.5 adapter (`Dynamics(action_dim=7, action_num=15)`), into `assert action_cond.shape[1:] == (num_frames+num_history, action_dim)`, and into the `cartesian_pose`/`gripper_pose` concatenation at `dataset_droid_exp33.py:192`. Changing it means retraining and touching four files.
- **New camera / view count**: `cond_cam_id1/2/3 = 0/1/2` are literals at `dataset/dataset_droid_exp33.py:177-179` and the three latents are concatenated by hand. Two views or four views is a code change, not a config change. This is the sharpest contrast with OpenDWM and Genie-Envisioner, both of which take a view *list*.
- **New policy**: subclass the pattern in `forward_policy` (`scripts/rollout_interact_pi.py:227-257`). You need (a) something that returns an action chunk from `{images, state, joints, prompt}` and (b) an adapter to Cartesian pose if your policy speaks another language.
- **Coupling hotspots**: `config.py` is a single 260-line dataclass mixing training hyperparameters, model architecture, rollout settings and per-task magic constants, with `__post_init__` branching on `task_type` to select hardcoded trajectory IDs and start indices. Any new task means editing that chain. The four `scripts/rollout_*.py` files duplicate ~60 lines of setup each.

## Hardware and cost

Training: "one/two nodes each with 8 A100/H100 cards" (README section 0), `train_batch_size = 4`, fp16 mixed precision, 500k max steps, checkpoints every 20k. The published checkpoint is `checkpoint-10000.pt`, so ~10k steps of fine-tuning on top of SVD.

Inference: no VRAM figure is published. The model is SVD-scale (~1.5 B UNet) in bf16 over 3 views x 11 frames at 320x192 with 50 denoising steps — a 24 GB card is plausible for the world model alone, but the π0.5 loop co-hosts a JAX policy on the same GPU and the README tells you to set `XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_MEM_FRACTION=0.4` to stop JAX pre-allocating, which implies an 80 GB A100/H100 for the combined loop. **Speed is stated**: "one interaction step takes around ~10s on A100 or ~5s on H100" for 1 second of generated video across three views, i.e. **5-10x slower than real time**. A 12-step rollout is 1-2 minutes; the paper's 20-episodes-x-6-tasks sweep is therefore a multi-hour single-GPU job.

## Verdict and next step

**TRIAL — take the harness, not the model.** Ctrl-World is a small, honest, MIT-licensed research repo that does one thing very well: it shows that you can evaluate a real VLA policy in a learned world model and get a signal that tracks reality, and it ships the whole loop including the awkward part (a learned adapter between the policy's action representation and the world model's). For roboticarray that pattern is directly reusable as a cheap pre-screen ahead of Isaac Lab. The model itself is not: it is welded to DROID's three-camera Franka rig, a 7-D Cartesian action space, 5 Hz and 320x192, with per-task gripper clamps that reveal a narrow competence envelope, and it never produces a control. The licence is also worse than it first looks — MIT code and an "MIT" checkpoint sitting on top of a Stability AI Community License base with a $1M revenue cap, which for us means talking to Stability or rebasing on a permissive video backbone. Concrete next step, about a week: run `scripts/rollout_replay_traj.py` on the bundled `droid_subset` to confirm the stack works end to end, then rewrite `scripts/rollout_interact_pi.py`'s `forward_policy` against one of our own policies to prove the adapter pattern generalises. If that works, the real project is not adopting this checkpoint but rebuilding the same loop on a permissively-licensed backbone (Cosmos or LTX) with Isaac-rendered training data and our own action space — at which point `config.py` and the four duplicated rollout scripts should be thrown away and rewritten properly.
