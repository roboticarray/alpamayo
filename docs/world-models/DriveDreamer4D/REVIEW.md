## What it is

DriveDreamer4D is a fork of [DriveStudio](https://github.com/ziyc/drivestudio) with a paper-specific evaluation path bolted on. The paper's idea is to use a video world model (DriveDreamer-2, from the same group, unreleased) as a "data machine": synthesise videos along novel ego trajectories such as lane changes and speed changes, merge them with the real recorded data via a "cousin data training strategy", and fit a 4D Gaussian-splatting scene that then renders correctly off the recorded driving line. **What is actually in this repo is only the 4DGS half**: DriveStudio's dataset loaders for six driving datasets, its Gaussian models (Periodic Vibration Gaussians, deformable GS, vanilla, SMPL nodes), a single `tools/eval.py` entry point, a set of hand-written novel-trajectory generators in `utils/camera.py`, and the paper's two novel-trajectory metrics (NTA-IoU and NTL-IoU). There is **no `tools/train.py`** — the repository as published cannot fit a scene, only render and score a scene someone else fitted. There is no world model, no synthetic-data generation, and no cousin-data merging code.

## Physics and dynamics

- **No dynamics model exists here.** A 4DGS scene is an appearance-and-geometry fit. Motion of other agents is represented either by Periodic Vibration Gaussians (a time-varying Gaussian field, `models/gaussians/pvg.py`) or by rigid/deformable node models, all fitted per scene. Nothing predicts, nothing simulates, nothing is conditioned on an action.
- **Novel-trajectory format and how it is generated.** `utils/camera.py:58-84` dispatches on a `traj_type` string to one of `front_center_interp`, `s_curve`, `three_key_poses`, `acc`, `dec`, `change_lane_left`, `change_lane_right`, `interp`, `bias`. The implementations are as simple as they sound. `change_lane_left` (`utils/camera.py:159-174`) interpolates the recorded front-camera poses to the target frame count and then does `cam_pose[1,3] += 0.1*i` for each frame `i` — a linearly growing lateral offset in the world frame. `acc_traj` (`camera.py:133-147`) does the same on `cam_pose[0,3]`. So an "acceleration manoeuvre" is a fixed 0.1 m per-frame longitudinal shift and a "lane change" is a fixed 0.1 m per-frame lateral drift, **with the camera orientation left untouched** — the vehicle slides sideways without ever turning its heading. No kinematic model, no curvature limit, no yaw. Over a 40-frame clip the lane change accumulates to 4 m of lateral offset, which is about right for a lane width, so the *result* looks reasonable; the *method* has no physics in it.
- **Two in-place bugs worth knowing.** These generators mutate the pose tensors they received from `per_cam_poses[0]` rather than copying, so calling two trajectory types in one run compounds the offsets. And `dec_traj` (`camera.py:149-157`) omits the `target_frames > len(cam_poses)` interpolation branch that all its siblings have, so it silently ignores the requested frame count.
- **Resolution, fps, horizon.** Waymo front camera at 1920x1280, loaded at full size then `downscale: 2` for training (`configs/datasets/waymo/1cams.yaml`), single camera (`cameras: [0]`) in the shipped config although the loader supports up to five. Waymo is 10 Hz; the example clip is `start_timestep: 120` to `end_timestep: 159`, i.e. **40 frames ≈ 4 seconds**. Novel-view rendering defaults to `fps: 30` for the output video, which is an interpolation artifact, not new information. This is a 4-second, single-camera, single-scene artifact.
- **Evaluation.** PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS on held-out frames (`tools/eval.py:50-62`, including `human_psnr` / `vehicle_psnr` breakdowns, which is a nice touch inherited from DriveStudio) plus the paper's two contributions: **NTA-IoU** (`utils/metrics/NTA-IoU/get_NTA-IoU.py`) runs a YOLO11 detector on the novel-trajectory render and measures IoU against the ground-truth 3D boxes projected into the novel view, and **NTL-IoU** (`utils/metrics/NTL-IoU/get_NTL-IoU.py`) runs TwinLiteNet lane segmentation and measures lane-line IoU. These are the most interesting thing in the repo: they are a way to ask "did the scene stay geometrically correct when we moved off the recorded line?" without a human looking at it. Both require external detector weights you must supply yourself (`--model_path /PATH/TO/YOUR/YOLO11/MODEL`), and neither is bundled.
- **Known failure modes.** Not documented. Structurally: 3DGS degrades quickly as the viewpoint leaves the training distribution (this is the problem the paper exists to address, and the paper's fix is the unreleased half); the lateral-slide trajectories produce physically impossible ego motion that a perception model trained on real data may find odd; dynamic agents under PVG are the weakest part of any such reconstruction.

## Motion-control assessment

The trajectory interface is, ironically, the cleanest match to Alpamayo in this batch. `get_novel_render_traj` (`datasets/driving_dataset.py:709-744`) returns and `prepare_novel_view_render_data` (`driving_dataset.py:746-759`) consumes a tensor of shape **`(target_frames, 4, 4)`** — a plain sequence of camera-to-world matrices. Alpamayo's `ActionSpace.action_to_traj` (`src/alpamayo_r1/action_space/action_space.py:47-70`) returns `traj_future_xyz (..., T, 3)` and `traj_future_rot (..., T, 3, 3)`, which assemble into exactly that: `T[:, :3, :3] = traj_future_rot`, `T[:, :3, 3] = traj_future_xyz`, bottom row `[0,0,0,1]`, then compose with the ego-to-camera extrinsic. No quantisation, no unit conversion, no lost degrees of freedom — and unlike the hand-written generators, an Alpamayo trajectory would carry a real heading, so the rendered camera would actually turn during a lane change instead of sliding. Registering a `"alpamayo"` entry in the `trajectory_generators` dict at `utils/camera.py:67-79` that reads a trajectory from disk is a ten-line change.

That is genuinely the shape of a useful tool for us: fit a Gaussian scene from a real drive, then render what Alpamayo's planner *would have seen* had it taken a different line, and score it with NTA-IoU. It is the sensor-realism counterpart to AlpaSim's dynamics. But three things stop this specific repository from being the vehicle for it. First, there is no training script, so we cannot fit our own scenes with it — we would be limited to re-rendering the one Waymo clip they published. Second, the licence: no grant on the first-party code, and `models/modules.py:10` imports nvdiffrast at module scope, whose NVIDIA licence limits us to research and evaluation. Third, the actual contribution — using a generative world model to fill in the off-trajectory supervision — is not here, so we would be reproducing baseline 4DGS, which degrades exactly where we need it.

The right move is therefore to go to the parent project. **DriveStudio** has the training script, the same six dataset loaders, the same Gaussian models, the same `(T,4,4)` novel-view interface, and an actual licence. Use it for the reconstruct-and-rerender capability, and port the NTA-IoU / NTL-IoU idea (not the code, which is unlicensed) as the off-trajectory quality gate. Relative to Isaac Sim, this whole class of tool is complementary rather than competing: Isaac gives us a controllable world with ground-truth physics and imperfect appearance; 4DGS gives us perfect appearance of a real world with no controllability. Neither is a closed loop on its own.

## Extensibility

- **New dataset**: the DriveStudio pattern, and it is a good one. Add `datasets/<name>/<name>_preprocess.py` (raw → the common on-disk layout), `<name>_sourceloader.py` with a `PixelSource` and a `LiDARSource` subclass, register the dataset name in `datasets/dataset_meta.py`, and add a `configs/datasets/<name>/Ncams.yaml`. Six datasets already follow it, so the contract is well exercised.
- **New sensor / camera count**: `cameras: [0]` in the dataset YAML is a list; the Waymo loader documents indices 0-4 and nuPlan ships an 8-camera config. Rendering more views is a config change, not a code change — this is the only repo in the batch where multi-camera is free, because a Gaussian scene is view-independent by construction.
- **New trajectory type / action space**: add a function to `utils/camera.py` and a key to the `trajectory_generators` dict at `camera.py:67-79`. Copy the poses before mutating them, unlike the existing implementations.
- **New Gaussian / scene model**: `models/gaussians/` holds `vanilla.py`, `pvg.py`, `deformgs.py`, `basics.py`; `models/nodes/` holds `rigid.py`, `deformable.py`, `smpl.py`. Selected by `type:` in the model section of the config. This is a real plugin point.
- **Coupling hotspots**: `tools/eval.py:4` pins the process to GPU 0 before torch is imported; `models/modules.py:10` makes nvdiffrast a hard import for the whole `models` package even when SMPL is off (`load_smpl: False` in the shipped config); `models/human_body.py:19-21` imports `third_party.smplx.smplx`, a path that does not exist in this tree; the absent `tools/train.py` means the config's entire `trainer.optim` / `losses` / `gaussian_ctrl_general_cfg` sections are dead weight in this repository.

## Hardware and cost

**Upstream states nothing** — no VRAM figure, no GPU count, no timing, anywhere in the README or the configs. What can be inferred: `preload_device: cuda` in `configs/datasets/waymo/1cams.yaml` caches the entire decoded clip on the GPU, which for 40 frames of 1920x1280 at `downscale: 2` plus LiDAR and masks is a few GB before any Gaussians exist; `num_iters: 30000` with `stop_split_at: 20000` is a standard 3DGS schedule that typically lands in the 10-24 GB range for a driving scene of this size, on one GPU (the code is single-GPU only — `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES='0'` is hardcoded at `tools/eval.py:4`). Call it **24 GB, one GPU, unverified** — and note that since no training script ships, the only cost we can actually incur is rendering, which is cheap. The real cost is the build: `gsplat-1.3.0`, `nvdiffrast` and `pytorch3d` are all CUDA extensions compiled from source against a pinned torch, on Python 3.8, with a `requirements.txt` that pins `xformers==0.0.18` against a torch 2.4.1 install. Budget hours for the environment, not for the compute.

## Verdict and next step

**SKIP.** This repository is the evaluation harness for a paper whose actual contribution — the world model that manufactures off-trajectory training video, and the cousin-data strategy that merges it with real data — was not released. Without those, and without a training script, what you have is DriveStudio with a worse licence: no `LICENSE` file at all on the first-party code, an unconditional import of nvdiffrast whose NVIDIA terms confine us to research and evaluation, SMPL-X under a non-commercial MPI licence, and a "checkpoint" that is one Waymo scene distributed over Baidu Pan. Do not invest here. The next step, if the reconstruct-and-re-render capability is wanted, is to evaluate **DriveStudio** (ziyc/drivestudio) directly — it is the parent of this code, it has the training script this fork removed, and it exposes the same `(T,4,4)` novel-trajectory interface that maps onto Alpamayo's `traj_future_xyz` / `traj_future_rot` without loss. From this repo, take exactly one idea forward: NTA-IoU and NTL-IoU, run a detector and a lane segmenter on the off-trajectory render and score them against the projected ground truth, is a cheap and sensible automated gate for "did the reconstruction survive moving off the recorded line", and we should build our own version of it for whatever neural-rendering stack we end up with. Re-check this repo only if GigaAI releases DriveDreamer-2 and a licence.
