## What it is

DreamerV3 is model-based reinforcement learning: an RSSM world model encodes observations into categorical latents and predicts future latents, rewards and episode continuation given actions, and an actor-critic is trained entirely on *imagined* rollouts inside that model (`imag_length: 15`, `horizon: 333`). This is the author's own JAX reimplementation (version 3.3.1, actively maintained), organized as two packages: `dreamerv3/` (agent, RSSM, configs, main) and `embodied/` (environment interface, replay, drivers, distributed runners, and a thin JAX layer over ninjax/optax). The headline claim, backed by the Nature paper, is that a **single fixed hyperparameter set** masters Atari, DMLab, DMC proprio and vision, Crafter, Procgen, BSuite, Minecraft diamond and quadruped locomotion — model sizes are selected by a one-line config block from `size1m` to `size400m` (default `size200m`: RSSM deter 8192, 64 stochastic categoricals of 64 classes, 1024 units). Unlike every other repo here it is not a pretrained perception-based world model to plug in; it is an algorithm you point at an environment.

## Security findings

- `Dockerfile:26` — `RUN wget -O - https://gist.githubusercontent.com/danijar/ca6ab917188d2e081a8253b3ca5c36d3/raw/install-dmlab.sh | sh`. A pipe-to-shell from a **personal gist raw URL with no revision pin**: the content can change under us at any time, and it runs as root during image build. This is only needed for DMLab; delete the line if you do not use it, or pin the gist revision SHA in the URL and vendor the script.
- `entrypoint.sh:10-13` — four `curl` calls to `http://metadata.google.internal/...` for GCP instance metadata, printed to the log. Harmless outside GCP (they fail to `NA`) but it is an unexpected outbound call in an entrypoint; strip it in a fork.
- `requirements.txt` pins `jax[cuda12]==0.4.33` and `nvidia-cuda-nvcc-cu12<=12.2`, while `Dockerfile:41` installs `jax[cuda]==0.5.0` before installing those requirements — the image ends up with whichever pip resolves last. Version skew between JAX, jaxlib, the CUDA pip wheels and the host driver is the single most common failure mode of this repo, and upstream contradicting itself makes it worse. Pin one JAX version in the fork and delete the other.
- `requirements.txt` also carries `autorom[accept-rom-license]==0.6.1`, which downloads Atari ROMs and auto-accepts their licence at install time. Remove it unless Atari is actually needed.
- **Positive:** no `pickle`, no `eval`/`exec`, no `os.system`, no `subprocess`, no `torch.load`, no `trust_remote_code`, no hardcoded tokens anywhere in the Python tree. Checkpointing goes through `elements.Checkpoint` (`embodied/run/train.py:83-90`) rather than raw pickles. Most dependencies are pinned or floored.
- Note for deployment: `run.script: parallel` and the `parallel_*` scripts bind TCP sockets (`actor_addr`, `replay_addr`, `logger_addr` default to `localhost:{auto}`) via `portal`. Defaults are loopback; be deliberate before exposing those on a shared host.

## Physics and dynamics

- **Action spaces:** both, and mixed. Continuous actions are normalized and clipped by wrappers applied automatically in `wrap_env` (`main.py:249-257`: `NormalizeAction`, `UnifyDtypes`, `CheckSpaces`, `ClipAction`); the policy head picks `categorical` for discrete and `bounded_normal` for continuous per action key (`agent.py:60-63`, `policy_dist_disc`/`policy_dist_cont`). Action spaces are **dicts**, so multi-part actions (e.g. joint targets plus a gripper, or Minecraft's camera plus buttons) are native. `FromGym` flattens nested gym `Dict` spaces automatically.
- **World model:** RSSM with an 8192-dim deterministic recurrent state and 32x64 categorical stochastic latents (`configs.yaml` `agent.dyn.rssm`), trained with reconstruction, reward, continuation, dynamics and representation losses (`loss_scales`). Observations may be images (CNN with `depth: 64`, mults `[2,3,4,4]`) and/or vectors, symlog-transformed.
- **Horizon:** imagination rollouts are 15 steps (`imag_length: 15`) with a 333-step discount horizon; training batches are 16 sequences of 64 steps (`batch_size: 16`, `batch_length: 64`).
- **Resolution / rate:** 64x64 (96x96 for Atari/Procgen) with per-suite action repeat (`repeat: 4` for Atari/DMLab, `1` for DMC and loconav). There is no absolute frame rate — the environment defines it.
- **Evaluation:** episode return, nothing else. `scores/*.json.gz` hold the published curves for DreamerV3 and baselines across eight benchmark suites, and `plot.py` regenerates the comparison figures. There is no dynamics-error metric, no physical-consistency check, no closed-loop transfer study.
- **Failure modes:** the world model is only as good as the reward-relevant parts of the data distribution, so it models what the policy visits and nothing else; latent dynamics have no physical grounding, so a learned model will happily violate conservation laws off-distribution; and the method is sample-hungry by robotics standards (`atari` config: 5.1e7 environment steps; `dmc_vision`: 1.1e6 steps at `train_ratio: 256`).

## Motion-control assessment

DreamerV3 is a *learner*, which makes its relationship to our stack completely different from the other four repos. It would consume an Isaac Lab task, not provide a model for one. Integration is honestly not hard: implement `embodied.Env` with `obs_space`, `act_space` (dicts of `elements.Space`) and `step(action) -> obs dict`, add one entry to the `ctor` map in `main.py:217-234`, and the wrappers, replay, driver and distributed runner come free. The pragmatic route is `embodied.envs.from_gym.FromGym`, but be aware it speaks the **pre-0.26 gym API** — `obs = env.reset()` and a 4-tuple `step` return (`from_gym.py:60-68`) — whereas Isaac Lab is Gymnasium, so you need a ~30-line adapter for the 5-tuple/`terminated`+`truncated` split. The deeper mismatch is vectorization: Isaac Lab's value proposition is thousands of parallel envs in one process on the GPU, while `embodied` scales by running `run.envs: 16` separate environment instances and, in `parallel` mode, separate processes talking over `portal` sockets. Feeding a single Isaac Lab vectorized env into DreamerV3 efficiently means writing a batched `embodied.Env` and checking the driver handles it — that is the main engineering risk in any spike. Is it practical as a motion-control learner for us? For *research* on tasks where reward is cheap to define and sample efficiency matters (contact-rich manipulation, recovery behaviours, locomotion on novel morphologies), yes: the fixed-hyperparameter claim is the real selling point, since it removes the tuning treadmill that makes PPO/SAC baselines expensive. For AV motion control, no: driving policies are trained on logged data with imitation and safety constraints, not by 5e7 steps of reward-seeking interaction, and DreamerV3 offers no mechanism for constraint satisfaction or for the trajectory-level cost structure that AlpaSim planning uses. Its most credible near-term role for roboticarray is as a strong baseline to beat on Isaac Lab manipulation tasks, and as a source of one specific idea — training a policy on imagined latent rollouts — rather than as a component.

## Extensibility

- **New environment:** subclass `embodied.Env` (see `embodied/envs/dummy.py` for the minimal example, `dmc.py` for a realistic one), then add a suite key to the `ctor` dict at `main.py:217-234`. Per-suite kwargs come from the `env:` block in `configs.yaml`, so `--env.myenv.size '[64,64]'` works immediately.
- **Wrapping an existing env:** `FromGym` (old gym API) and `FromDM` (dm_env API) are the adapters. Both flatten dict spaces and handle the `is_first`/`is_last`/`is_terminal` bookkeeping DreamerV3 needs.
- **New action space:** nothing to do — spaces are read from the environment and the policy head branches on `space.discrete` (`agent.py:61`).
- **New sensor:** add a key to `obs_space`; the encoder routes image-shaped keys to the CNN and everything else to the MLP (`agent.enc.simple`). No code change needed for extra proprio channels.
- **Model size / compute:** `--configs <task> size25m` style blocks apply regex overrides across the whole config tree (`configs.yaml` `size1m`...`size400m`), which is the cleanest scaling knob of any repo reviewed.
- **Coupling hotspots:** two registry entries are dead — `'dm': 'embodied.envs.from_dmenv:FromDM'` (`main.py:220`) points at a module that does not exist (the file is `embodied/envs/from_dm.py`) and `'langroom': 'embodied.envs.langroom:LangRoom'` (`main.py:229`) has no corresponding file at all. Both raise `ModuleNotFoundError` only when that suite is selected. `FromGym`'s old-gym assumption is the other hotspot. `jax.prealloc: True` means JAX grabs most of the GPU on start, which interacts badly with a simulator sharing the same device — set `--jax.prealloc False` when running alongside Isaac Sim.

## Hardware and cost

Upstream states no VRAM figures (estimates below are ours). The default `size200m` configuration at `batch_size: 16` x `batch_length: 64` in bfloat16 wants a 40-80 GB card; `size12m`/`size25m` fit comfortably on 16-24 GB, and `--configs debug` runs on a laptop CPU (`--jax.platform cpu`). The reference runs are single-GPU: `jax.policy_devices: [0]`, `train_devices: [0]`, with multi-GPU and multi-node available through `replicas`/`JOB_COMPLETION_INDEX` and the `parallel` script. Wall-clock is dominated by the environment and the train ratio: the `atari` config is 5.1e7 environment steps at `train_ratio: 32`, i.e. roughly 1.6e9 replayed transitions, about 1.6M gradient steps at the default batch — a multi-day single-GPU run per task. Data-efficient settings trade the other way (`crafter`: 1.1e6 steps at `train_ratio: 512`). Budget one GPU per task for days, not hours, and remember `--jax.prealloc False` if the simulator shares the device.

## Verdict and next step

TRIAL, with clear scope. This is the best-engineered repo in the batch — MIT, actively maintained, tested, no pickle surface, a fixed hyperparameter set that genuinely removes tuning work, and the broadest demonstrated domain coverage — but it answers a different question from the other four. It gives us no pretrained perception, no planner and nothing zero-shot; it gives us a strong model-based RL learner that will consume whatever simulator we hand it. The realistic value is as a research baseline for Isaac Lab motion-control tasks and as a source of the imagination-training idea, not as a component of an AV planning stack. Recommended spike (about one week): fork, delete the gist pipe at `Dockerfile:26` and the GCP curls in `entrypoint.sh`, resolve the JAX pin conflict to a single version, then write `embodied/envs/isaaclab.py` wrapping one Isaac Lab manipulation task (starting from `from_gym.py` plus a Gymnasium 5-tuple shim) and train `size25m` on a single GPU with `--jax.prealloc False`. Two things to measure: whether the driver keeps a vectorized Isaac env busy, and sample efficiency versus whatever PPO baseline that task already has. If the vectorized-env integration turns out to be a rewrite of `embodied/core/driver.py`, stop — that is the point where the cost outgrows the benefit.
