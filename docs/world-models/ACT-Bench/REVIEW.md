## What it is

ACT-Bench is two things in one repo. The first is a **benchmark for action controllability**: you generate 2286 videos from the ACT-Bench dataset's trajectory instructions, drop the MP4s in a directory, and `run_benchmark.py` runs them through ACT-Estimator — a small (20.5M) inverse-dynamics model that watches a driving video and predicts both the ego trajectory and which of nine manoeuvre classes was executed. It reports maneuver-classification accuracy plus ADE and FDE between the estimated and commanded trajectory (`act_bench/metrics.py`). Crucially the harness is **model-agnostic**: it only consumes MP4 files, so it can score Vista, ReSim, OpenDWM or anything we build, and the repo ships pre-generated videos from the paper so the numbers reproduce without a GPU. The second thing is **Terra**, Turing's baseline world model: a VQ image tokenizer feeding an autoregressive LLaMA-style transformer with interleaved action tokens (576 image tokens per frame, `Terra/generate.py:24`), decoded either directly or through an SVD-derived video refiner, with an optional vLLM fast path. Terra generates video continuations from ~3 context frames plus per-frame trajectory instructions and claims high trajectory adherence. Code quality is the best in this batch — `uv.lock`, a strict ruff config, pandera schema validation — but the weights are CC-BY-NC-SA and nothing has been touched since December 2024.

## Security findings

- `act_bench/compute_score.py:254` — `AutoModel.from_pretrained("turing-motors/Act-Estimator", trust_remote_code=True)`. The HF repo carries the `custom_code` tag, so this downloads and **executes** modelling Python from the Hub every time you score a run. This is the core of the benchmark; there is no `trust_remote_code=False` path.
- `Terra/generate.py:312` and `:373` — two more `trust_remote_code=True` loads (`AutoModel`/`AutoConfig` for `turing-motors/Terra`).
- `Terra/vllm_impl/modeling_llama_action.py:57` — `AutoConfig.from_pretrained(..., trust_remote_code=True)` executed **at module import time**, i.e. merely importing the vLLM implementation fetches and runs remote code.
- `Terra/video_refiner/models/diffusion.py:72` — a fourth `trust_remote_code=True`.
- `Terra/generate.py:171` — `torch.load(weights_path, weights_only=False, map_location="cpu")` on the video-refiner checkpoint, explicitly opting out of the safe loader.
- `Terra/video_refiner/models/diffusion.py:117` and `:119` — two more `torch.load(..., weights_only=False)`.
- `Terra/video_refiner/` is an SVD/`generative-models` fork and inherits its `instantiate_from_config` pattern (used at `Terra/generate.py:170`): YAML `target:` strings are imported and called, so `Terra/configs/video_refiner/inference.yaml` is executable content.
- `Terra/checkpoints/download_weights.sh` — benign: `source ../../.venv/bin/activate` then a single `huggingface-cli download`. No curl-pipe-sh anywhere in the repo, no `os.system`, no `subprocess`, no `eval`/`exec`, no hardcoded tokens.
- **Good news on dependencies:** a complete `uv.lock` (427 KB) pins the entire transitive graph with hashes, and `pyproject.toml` pins several packages exactly. This is the only repo in the batch with a real lockfile, and it substantially offsets the supply-chain risk *below* the `trust_remote_code` layer.
- Net assessment: the dependency story is the best here and the runtime-code story is the worst. If we use this, mirror both HF repos, pin them to a reviewed revision, read the `custom_code` modelling files once, and load from the local mirror.

## Physics and dynamics

- **Action space (Terra)**: per generated frame, 6 action tokens (`N_ACTION_TOKENS = 6`, `Terra/generate.py:27`), each a `(y, x, t)` triple — lateral offset, longitudinal offset, and an explicit timestamp. `prepare_action` (`Terra/generate.py:146-165`) takes a 60-point trajectory sampled at 0.05 s (`np.arange(0.0, 3.0, 0.05)`), slices `[9::10][:6]`, and pairs each point with its time, giving waypoints at t ≈ 0.45, 0.95, 1.45, 1.95, 2.45, 2.95 s. So: **6 waypoints over a 3-second horizon at ~0.5 s spacing, with time explicit in the token**. Note `action[:, [1, 0]]` at `:161` swaps the coordinate order — get this wrong and the model steers sideways.
- **Action vocabulary (benchmark)**: nine classes (`act_bench/compute_score.py:32-42`) — `curving_to_left`, `curving_to_right`, `straight_constant_high_speed`, `straight_constant_low_speed`, `straight_accelerating`, `straight_decelerating`, `starting`, `stopping`, `stopped`. A sane, control-flavoured taxonomy that covers longitudinal and lateral behaviour, though nothing aggressive (no lane change, no swerve, no emergency brake).
- **Horizon / resolution / fps**: generation is capped at `MAX_NUM_FRAMES = 50` (`generate.py:23`) and the README uses `--num_frames 47`; output is written at **10 fps** (`save_images_to_mp4`, `generate.py:132`), so ~4.7 s. Rollout is autoregressive in rounds of `N_FRAMES_PER_ROUND = 25` with configurable overlap. The scorer expects 44 frames (`num_frames = 44  # (25 - 3) * 2 round`, `compute_score.py:58`) resized to 224x224. Terra's native tokenizer grid is 576 tokens per frame (24x24). The video refiner uses `fps_id: 9` (`generate.py:271`), the SVD convention for 10 fps.
- **Evaluation**: manoeuvre accuracy (predicted vs commanded class), mean ADE and mean FDE in metres (`act_bench/metrics.py`), plus a confusion-matrix plot. This is the only dynamics-quantitative evaluation across all four repos.
- **Known failure modes**: ACT-Estimator is itself a learned model, so the metric has a ceiling and an unmeasured bias — a world model could in principle satisfy the estimator without being physically right. The taxonomy conflates "stopping" and "stopped" in ways that will be noisy at boundaries. Terra's 3 s instruction horizon against a 4.7 s rollout means the tail of every video is effectively unconditioned. And 5 minutes per sample makes the full 2286-sample benchmark an 8-day single-GPU job.

## Motion-control assessment

The valuable object here is **ACT-Estimator, not Terra**. Every other world model in this batch shares one blocking limitation for closed-loop policy evaluation: they emit pixels and no state, so you cannot tell where the ego vehicle actually went. ACT-Estimator is exactly the inverse-dynamics model that closes that gap — video in, ego trajectory and manoeuvre class out. Wire it to Vista's or ReSim's output and you get a measurable loop: command Alpamayo's trajectory into the world model, render, estimate the realised trajectory, and compare. That is the single most transferable idea across these four reviews, and it is 20.5M parameters, which means training our own on our own data is a days-not-months project.

For Alpamayo compatibility, Terra's format is a good match with one wrinkle. Alpamayo-R1's `ActionSpace.action_to_traj` (`/home/user/alpamayo/src/alpamayo_r1/action_space/action_space.py`) returns `traj_future_xyz (..., T, 3)`; Terra wants 6 points of `(y, x, t)` over 3 s. Since Alpamayo's trajectories are already time-parameterised, the adapter is a resample to the six fixed timestamps plus the coordinate swap at `generate.py:161`. Heading is unused, so `traj_future_rot` is discarded — less faithful than ReSim or OpenDWM. The benchmark side needs no adapter at all: it consumes MP4s, so it can score any of our pipelines as-is.

The blocker is legal, and it is hard. CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0 is both non-commercial *and* viral: derivatives must be shared under the same terms. Using ACT-Estimator to evaluate a model we intend to commercialise is, at best, a question for counsel; distributing anything derived from it is out of the question. The Apache-2.0 code does not help, because the code is a thin shell around NC weights. The realistic path is therefore to treat this repo as a **specification**: adopt the nine-class taxonomy and the accuracy/ADE/FDE protocol, read the estimator's architecture, and train our own estimator on our own (or on permissively licensed) data. That we can then run against Vista, ReSim, OpenDWM and AlpaSim without a licence question.

Against the Isaac/Unreal stack there is a second use: an inverse-dynamics estimator trained on Isaac Sim renders, where ground-truth ego trajectory is free, would be cheap to produce and directly reusable — Isaac gives us unlimited labelled (video, trajectory) pairs, which is precisely ACT-Estimator's training signal.

## Extensibility

- **Scoring a new world model**: no integration at all — write 2286 MP4s named `NUSCENES_ACTION_*.mp4` into a directory and run `run_benchmark.py --input_dir ... --output_dir ...`, or call `compute_score(ActBenchConfig(...))` from Python. This is the cleanest extension point in any of the four repos, and it is why the repo is worth reading even if we never run Terra.
- **New manoeuvre class**: hardcoded at `act_bench/compute_score.py:32-42`, and the estimator's output head is sized to it. Adding "lane change left" means retraining ACT-Estimator and regenerating the benchmark dataset's instruction trajectories. This is the main ceiling on the benchmark.
- **New action format for Terra**: `prepare_action` (`Terra/generate.py:146`) is a single self-contained function with all the sampling constants (`PATH_START_ID = 9`, `PATH_POINT_INTERVAL = 10`, `N_ACTION_TOKENS = 6`) as module-level globals at `:25-27`. Changing the waypoint schedule is a small edit; changing the *token* format requires retraining the autoregressive model.
- **New dataset**: the benchmark's data contract is a JSONL with `context_frames`, `instruction_trajs`, `cond_label`, `cond_class` (plus unused `reference_traj`, `intrinsic`), validated by pandera schemas in `act_bench/`. Documented on the HF dataset card and easy to emit from our own logs or from Isaac Sim.
- **Coupling hotspots**: the `trust_remote_code` loads hardcode HF repo ids (`compute_score.py:254`, `generate.py:312`), so pointing at a local mirror means editing source; `num_frames = 44` and `img_size = 224` are fixed in `ActBenchConfig.__post_init__`; `Terra/checkpoints/download_weights.sh` assumes `../../.venv` exists.

## Hardware and cost

The only repo in this batch that states real numbers. Generation: **~5 minutes per sample on a single H100 80 GB**, and the README does the arithmetic — 2286 samples is ~8 days on one GPU, so shard the JSONL across GPUs. The vLLM path (`uv pip install vllm==0.6.3.post1`, `--vllm_impl`) is what the paper used and is the intended speedup for the autoregressive transformer; the video refiner is the expensive half and can be dropped by omitting `--decoding_method video_refiner`, at a cost in fidelity. VRAM for Terra is not stated explicitly, but H100 80 GB is the reference and the SVD-derived refiner has the same appetite as Vista's. Scoring is cheap by comparison: ACT-Estimator is 20.5M parameters at 224x224 over 44 frames, with `batch_size: 10` and `num_workers: 32` defaults — a single mid-range GPU, minutes for the whole 2286-video set. That asymmetry matters: the *scorer* is essentially free to run, which is what makes it attractive as a component.

## Verdict and next step

**TRIAL — the method, not the weights.** The benchmark design is the most directly relevant idea in this whole review batch: a small inverse-dynamics model that turns generated video back into a trajectory, and a protocol (manoeuvre accuracy + ADE + FDE over a nine-class taxonomy) that makes "does this world model actually follow the action I gave it?" a number rather than an opinion. Every other repo here lacks exactly that, and it is the thing standing between a video world model and a usable closed loop around Alpamayo. But CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0 on both Terra and ACT-Estimator is a hard stop for commercial use, made worse by the share-alike clause, and the repo has been untouched since December 2024. Concrete next step: spend a short spike reading the ACT-Estimator architecture and the benchmark's JSONL contract, then train our own 20M-parameter action estimator on Isaac Sim renders (where ground-truth ego trajectory is free and licensing is ours), and stand up the accuracy/ADE/FDE protocol as an internal harness we can point at Vista, ReSim, OpenDWM and AlpaSim alike. Run the upstream benchmark itself only on the pre-generated paper videos, for calibration, and only under a research exemption confirmed by counsel. Do not build product on these weights, and if anyone does run the scorer, mirror both HF repos at a reviewed revision first — `trust_remote_code=True` at `act_bench/compute_score.py:254` means scoring a run executes third-party Python.
